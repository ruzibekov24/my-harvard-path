Miya · Uyqu

Imtihon oldidan tun bo'yi o'qish foydami?

Ertaga imtihon, kitob hali tugamagan — “bir kecha uxlamasam nima bo'pti?” Tadqiqotlar javobi aniq: uxlamagan miya o'qiganini yo'qotadi.

MyHarvardPath · ilmiy tadqiqotlar asosida · ≈ 3 daqiqa o'qish

Tasavvur qiling: kun bo'yi o'qigan narsalaringiz miyada vaqtinchalik “qoralama” sifatida turadi. Ularni uzoq muddatli xotiraga ko'chirish asosan tunda, chuqur uyqu paytida sodir bo'ladi. Olimlar buni xotiraning konsolidatsiyasi deb atashadi: miya kunduzgi taassurotlarni qayta “aylantirib”, po'stloqqa (korteksga) mustahkam joylashtiradi.

Tajriba: so'zlar va uyqu

2006-yilda nemis olimlari Gais, Lukas va Born maktab o'quvchilariga ingliz tilidagi so'zlarni yodlatishdi. O'rganganidan keyin bir necha soat ichida uxlagan o'quvchilar so'zlarni uzoq vaqt hushyor qolganlarga qaraganda yaxshiroq eslab qolishdi. O'rganilgan kechasi uxlamaganlar esa keyin to'yib uxlab olishganida ham orqada qolishdi.

Uxlamaslik — o'qiganingizni tunda saqlash tugmasini bosmasdan kompyuterni o'chirish bilan teng.

Eng xavflisi — o'zingizga ishonch

Uyqu bo'yicha tadqiqotchi Jun Pilcher laboratoriyasining ishlari qiziq bir narsani ko'rsatgan: uyqusiz odamlar natijasi yomonlashib borayotgan bo'lsa ham, o'zini yaxshi bajaryapman deb hisoblashda davom etadi. Ya'ni tun bo'yi o'qigan talaba ertalab “tayyorman” deb his qiladi — lekin xotira va diqqat aslida pasaygan bo'ladi.

20–30daqiqalik qisqa mashg'ulotlar — eng samarali
3–4kun oldin boshlash tavsiya etiladi
1to'liq tungi uyqu — imtihon oldidan majburiy

Unda qanday tayyorlanish kerak?

  • Oldindan boshlang. Texas A&M universiteti uyqu tadqiqotchisi Devid Ernest imtihondan 3–4 kun oldin kuniga bir necha marta 20–30 daqiqadan o'qishni maslahat beradi.
  • Odatdagi vaqtda yoting. Odatiy uxlash vaqtigacha o'qing, to'liq uxlang.
  • Ertalab takrorlang. Biroz erta turib, asosiy mavzularni yana bir bor ko'rib chiqing.
  • Yangi so'zlarni kechqurun yodlang. O'rganishdan keyin tez orada uxlash eslab qolishga yordam beradi.

SAT, IELTS yoki FLEX testidan oldingi kecha — yangi mavzu o'rganish vaqti emas. U vaqtda eng foydali “mashg'ulot” — uyqu.

Manbalar: Gais, Lucas, Born, “Sleep after learning aids memory recall”, Learning & Memory, 2006; Pilcher laboratoriyasi (Clemson universiteti) tadqiqotlari; Texas A&M universiteti, D. Earnest tavsiyalari (Sleep Review). Matn — o'zbek tilidagi erkin qisqacha bayon.

Brain · Sleep

Is pulling an all-nighter before an exam worth it?

The exam is tomorrow and the book is not finished: “what if I skip one night of sleep?” Research is clear: a brain that has not slept loses what it studied.

MyHarvardPath · Based on scientific research · ≈ 3 min read

Imagine that everything you studied during the day sits in your brain as a temporary “draft”. Moving it into long-term memory happens mainly at night, during deep sleep. Scientists call this memory consolidation: the brain “replays” the day's impressions and stores them firmly in the cortex.

An experiment: words and sleep

In 2006, German scientists Gais, Lucas and Born had school students memorise English vocabulary. Students who slept within a few hours of learning remembered the words better than those who stayed awake for a long time. Those who did not sleep on the night after learning stayed behind even after they later caught up on sleep.

Skipping sleep is like switching off the computer without pressing “save” on what you studied.

The most dangerous part: overconfidence

Work from sleep researcher June Pilcher's lab showed something interesting: sleep-deprived people keep believing they are doing well even as their performance gets worse. So a student who studied all night feels “ready” in the morning, while memory and attention have actually dropped.

20–30minute sessions are the most effective
3–4days ahead is when to start
1full night of sleep before the exam: essential

So how should you prepare?

  • Start early. Texas A&M sleep researcher David Earnest recommends studying 20–30 minutes at a time, several times a day, starting 3–4 days before the exam.
  • Go to bed at your usual time. Study until your normal bedtime, then get a full night's sleep.
  • Review in the morning. Get up a little earlier and go over the main topics once more.
  • Learn new words in the evening. Sleeping soon after learning helps you remember.

The night before the SAT, IELTS or FLEX test is not the time to learn new topics. At that point the most useful “practice” is sleep.

Sources: Gais, Lucas, Born, “Sleep after learning aids memory recall”, Learning & Memory, 2006; research from June Pilcher's lab (Clemson University); recommendations by D. Earnest, Texas A&M University (Sleep Review). Text: a free summary.
